# Round 2 — Airlock, silhouette, grounding

**Goal:** make the habitat read as one designed object, not a white capsule.
**Date:** 2026-10-24

## Reference study

- Igloo's `igloo-cube` and `igloo-detail` frames: one object, quiet surfaces, a few strong edges, no greeble noise.
- The Field Instrument cutaway and exterior: what a lunar pressure vessel needs to read as (airlock drum, berm, thermal hardware) and where it failed (giant glowing ring, floating props, soft key).

## The seven biggest weaknesses found

1. The airlock was a nearly hull-sized drum mating to a flat bolted plate — a jet-engine read.
2. The door was a black void with a small orange window.
3. The service spine was a brown MLI sausage on a white hull.
4. The solar array floated in the sky / read as scattered cards.
5. The legs were thin sticks; the hull sat on a flat pad with a black gap.
6. The hull was a plain tube: no radiator comb, no cupola, no mast silhouette.
7. The foreground was empty; the only vertical interest was the habitat.

## Changes

- Replaced the flat plate with a **64-segment lathe neck** from 3.34 m to 2.06 m; the drum now reads as a welded vestibule.
- Built a real pressure door: graphite leaf, heavy collar, six radial latches, handwheel, hinges, warm porthole, compact light bar and threshold. Removed the glowing donut.
- Replaced the brown spine with a low ceramic equipment housing, a single small gold MLI patch, four radiator fins, a dark-glass cupola with a warm ember, and a mast/dish/beacon.
- Added the near regolith berm and hid the stick legs; the lower hull now disappears into the ground.
- Added two flush curved access hatches with gaskets, handles and hinges.
- Tried a full solar array rebuild (cells, frames, mast, junction box). It still did not read at hero distance; it was later hidden.
- Added warm door lighting and a wider ground pool.

## Harsh critique

The airlock was better but the **door was still small inside a huge white face**. The face read as a ring with a porthole. The spine silhouette was an improvement, but the radiator fins were thin and the cupola read as a copper bowl. The solar array was still a failure: its frames blew out and the cells read black. The ground was smooth and over-lit; the berm helped grounding but looked like a snowdrift. The composition had scale, but the scene still felt like a clean 3D model, not a photograph.

The honest ranking of what still hurt: ground brightness > door proportion > solar array > material richness.

## Next focus

Kill the ground dunes and stipple, add a crater rim and rock field, lower the sun, and re-grade. Then move to the cutaway.
